The problem

Sound familiar?

Knowledge retires

The long-standing key user retires, resigns or is off sick. Handover: two weeks, minus holidays. Afterwards nobody knows how to post the credit memo or the return order.

76 emails instead of one process page

Decisions sit in email chains, PowerPoint, Excel and Teams folders. Newcomers read for days and still lack the big picture: who is upstream, who is downstream, who decides.

Three hours of video, no chapters

There are screen recordings, but they are long, outdated and stored in a hundred versions. New people don’t dare ask again, and experienced colleagues lose time to constant training.

From practice

Retirement without a single day of handover

Situation

International life sciences group, German site. The long-standing process owner retired without a handover. Knowledge was spread across PowerPoint, Excel and SharePoint; one open question hung on a chain of 76 emails.

Approach

Interviews with everyone along the process chain, one overall picture built from many partial views, upstream and downstream processes and owners clarified, viflow introduced and the processes published in the browser.

Result

Employees find processes and contacts themselves and have kept maintaining them since, instead of hunting for a person with every question.

Experience from my work as a process manager, anonymised. I have also seen first-hand, as a consultant and project member in several companies in Germany, how onboarding works without process knowledge: in automotive supply, energy, industry and IT.
